Ground the same way for three generations

Masala Manzil began in 1978 as a single stone grinder in a lane near Nayaganj, run by a spice merchant who refused to sell anything he hadn't tasted himself. Customers would bring their own chillies and turmeric from home, just to have them ground fresh in front of them — no shortcuts, no pre-mixing.

That same grinder still runs today, though it now shares the shop with two more. Every blend is roasted in small batches, cooled on cotton cloth, and ground only once the aroma is right — not by the clock, but by smell and feel, the way it has always been judged here.

Three generations later, the recipes haven't moved an inch. What's changed is simply how many kitchens they now reach.

The spice library

A short guide to what's behind the counter, and what each one is best known for.

Haldi (Turmeric)

Erode, Tamil Nadu

Sun-dried and stone-ground for a deep, earthy colour rather than the flat yellow of machine-milled turmeric.

Lal Mirch (Red Chilli)

Guntur, Andhra Pradesh

Chosen for colour as much as heat — sun-dried whole, then ground fine only after every stem is picked out by hand.

Dhania (Coriander)

Kota, Rajasthan

Lightly roasted before grinding, which is what gives our dhania powder its faint citrus note.

Jeera (Cumin)

Dry-roasted just until it darkens a shade — any further and the oils turn bitter instead of nutty.

How a blend is made

The same four stages, whether it's a kilo of turmeric or a batch of the house garam masala.

i.

Cleaning and sorting

Every batch is hand-sorted before roasting — stems, stones and broken pieces are picked out one by one.

ii.

Slow dry-roasting

Spices are roasted in small batches over a low flame, timed by aroma and colour rather than a clock.

iii.

Cooling and resting

Roasted spices rest on cotton cloth for a few hours so the oils settle before grinding.

iv.

Stone-grinding

Ground on the same style of stone mill the shop has used since 1978, in small batches, not large runs.

Where our spices travel from

Nearly every spice at Masala Manzil comes from a farming region known specifically for that one crop — Guntur for chilli, Unjha for cumin, Erode for turmeric. We buy directly where we can, rather than through a general wholesale mandi.

It means our stock changes slightly with the season, and a few blends sell out between harvests. We'd rather wait for the next batch than switch suppliers for convenience.

Getting the most from your masala

A few habits that make a real difference to how a spice tastes in the pan.

i.

Store away from light and heat

Keep powders in an airtight tin, away from the stove — heat and sunlight fade both colour and aroma fast.

ii.

Add whole spices to hot oil first

Cumin, mustard and whole garam masala release their flavour best when they hit hot oil before anything else goes in.

iii.

Add powders off direct heat

Turmeric and chilli powder scorch easily — add them once the pan has cooled slightly, or with a splash of water.

iv.

Buy little, buy often

Ground spice loses potency within a couple of months — a small jar bought more often beats a large one that sits half-used.

Good to know

A few things people usually ask before their first visit.

Do you grind spices to order?

Whole spices are ground fresh through the week in small batches. If you'd like a specific blend ground while you wait, call ahead and the shop will schedule it.

Are your spices tested for purity?

Every batch is checked for colour, aroma and texture before it goes on the shelf — the same way it has been judged since 1978, without machine additives to mask a poor batch.

Can I get a custom spice blend made?

Yes — many regulars have a family recipe they bring in, and the shop will roast and grind it to that exact ratio on request.

How long do your spices stay fresh?

Whole spices keep well for close to a year in an airtight container; ground blends are best used within two to three months for full aroma.

Do you ship outside Kanpur?

Call the shop directly — they can arrange packing and dispatch for orders outside the city on a case-by-case basis.
