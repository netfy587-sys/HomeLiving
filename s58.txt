<section class="story" id="story">
  <div class="wrap">
    <h2>Ground the same way for three generations</h2>
    <div class="story-body">
      <p>Masala Manzil began in 1978 as a single stone grinder in a lane near Nayaganj, run by a spice merchant who refused to sell anything he hadn't tasted himself. Customers would bring their own chillies and turmeric from home, just to have them ground fresh in front of them — no shortcuts, no pre-mixing.</p>
      <p>That same grinder still runs today, though it now shares the shop with two more. Every blend is roasted in small batches, cooled on cotton cloth, and ground only once the aroma is right — not by the clock, but by smell and feel, the way it has always been judged here.</p>
      <p>Three generations later, the recipes haven't moved an inch. What's changed is simply how many kitchens they now reach.</p>
      <div class="story-marks">
        <div class="story-mark"><strong>1978</strong><span>The shop opened its doors</span></div>

<section class="sourcing">
  <div class="wrap">
    <div>
      <h2>Where our spices travel from</h2>
      <div class="sourcing-body">
        <p>Nearly every spice at Masala Manzil comes from a farming region known specifically for that one crop — Guntur for chilli, Unjha for cumin, Erode for turmeric. We buy directly where we can, rather than through a general wholesale mandi.</p>
        <p>It means our stock changes slightly with the season, and a few blends sell out between harvests. We'd rather wait for the next batch than switch suppliers for convenience.</p>

  <div class="wrap">
    <div class="guide-head">
      <h2>Getting the most from your masala</h2>
      <p>A few habits that make a real difference to how a spice tastes in the pan.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="guide-list">
      <div class="guide-item">
        <div class="guide-num">i.</div>
        <div><h4>Store away from light and heat</h4><p>Keep powders in an airtight tin, away from the stove — heat and sunlight fade both colour and aroma fast.</p></div>
      </div>
      <div class="guide-item">
        <div class="guide-num">ii.</div>
        <div><h4>Add whole spices to hot oil first</h4><p>Cumin, mustard and whole garam masala release their flavour best when they hit hot oil before anything else goes in.</p></div>
      </div>
      <div class="guide-item">
        <div class="guide-num">iii.</div>
        <div><h4>Add powders off direct heat</h4><p>Turmeric and chilli powder scorch easily — add them once the pan has cooled slightly, or with a splash of water.</p></div>
      </div>
      <div class="guide-item">
        <div class="guide-num">iv.</div>
        <div><h4>Buy little, buy often</h4><p>Ground spice loses potency within a couple of months — a small jar bought more often beats a large one that sits half-used.</p></div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>
